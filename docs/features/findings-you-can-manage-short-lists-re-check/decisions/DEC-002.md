## Context

Anomaly findings were keyed by the observation id. Re-measuring a photograph (DEC-088) creates a new pair with new observations, so the same anomaly got a new key and a hidden one would come back after «Перепроверить все работы».

## Alternatives

1. Keep the observation id (marks are lost on every re-measure)
2. Key by engraving and layer (two photographs of one engraving would collide in one run)

## Decision

The key of an anomaly is `anomaly:photo:<original pair>:<layer index>`, where the original pair is found by following `correction_of` back to the first measurement of the photograph. A re-photograph (a new photo of the engraving) is new data and keeps its own key. A photograph that an old version accepted twice has two observations of one layer; the second one's key ends in `:2` (numbered in the order they were made). Runs made before the change keep their old keys; when two runs are compared (the report of «Перепроверить все работы»), an old key `anomaly:observation:<id>` is read as the photograph and layer of that observation, so the change of key is not reported as findings that went and came.

## Reason

A re-measure is the same photograph measured again; the operator's mark must follow it.

## Consequences

The history of an anomaly starts again at the first run after the change. `findings.compare_runs` maps old anomaly keys to the new form when it compares runs.
