## Context

Colour work is judged by ΔE to a target; a photo engraving has no single target colour. The tonal profile of a layer (16 greys → L*a*b*, ingest._tonal_profile) and the grey ramps of plates (_ramp_profile) already exist. The workshop's photo layers (pairs 106–119) show what goes wrong: reversed tones (a darker grey engraved lighter, pairs 112, 114, 117), blocked shadows (118: everything below grey 0.31 looks black), ranges of 3–50 L*.

## Alternatives

1. ΔE of each grey to an ideal grey scale: titanium's colours are not greys, the ideal is unknown
2. Rank correlation (rho) alone: it is 1 for a scale with only two distinguishable tones
3. SSIM between the picture and the photograph: one number that cannot say what to change

## Decision

photo_quality.tone(profile) gives: levels (greys told apart in the picture's order: a new tone each ≥ 2 ΔE76 from the last one and not darker than it by more than 1 L*), levels_share, range_L, black_L and white_L, shadows blocked and highlights lost (greys within 3 ΔE of an end), reversals (lightness turning back by more than 2 L*) and their share, mid_darkness (where the middle grey falls between the ends, 0.5 = the picture's own tone, above = darker mid-tones or dot gain; not given under 6 L* of range) and the mean L* (a picture's greys weighted by their area, a ramp's steps alike). photo_quality.detail(photo L*, expected L*, mask, px/mm) gives the share of the picture's contrast kept at 0.2, 0.4 and 0.8 mm (band-pass regression slope), the grain (residual ΔL*) and the same along and across the scan lines; the check measures it for every picture layer where its tonal profile was read (ingest._picture_detail). score = levels_share × (1 − reversal_share) × contrast × detail kept at 0.4 mm (capped at 1) when measured, where contrast = range_L / 40 L*, at most 1 (amended 2026-10-24: with every step told apart, a scale over 25 L* still looks pale beside one over 45 L*; without it the eight copies of a plate drawn lighter and darker all scored 1). The tone, score and findings are derived from the stored profile on every read (ingest.get_pair), never stored.

## Reason

Each number names something the operator sees and can change: too dark or too light, flat, blocked shadows, lost highlights, mid-tones gaining, blurred or grainy. Counting greys told apart rewards a long, ordered tonal scale, which is what a portrait needs; reversals break it most, so they multiply. A ramp at the same settings measures tone exactly, so the picture is left to measure detail.

## Consequences

Real layers (2026-10-24, with contrast): grayscale 119 0.92 and 116 0.79; Jarvis 118 (P25, 0.01 mm) 0.60 for its blocked shadows; grayscale 93 0.51 (every step told apart over 25 L* only); Jarvis 106 0.48; Atkinson 112 and Jarvis 114 near 0 for reversed tones. The score ranks engravings of one picture and the ramps of one plate; it is not a comparison across pictures.
