## Context

Settings of a dot kind interact with the picture's detail at its engraved size (a dot pattern at 0.01 mm against features of a face), and the operator tunes them by hand in LightBurn one engraving at a time. A grey-scales plate measures tone but not detail.

## Alternatives

1. The whole picture shrunk to the copy: the dots would meet a different picture than the work engraves
2. Pictures inside the plan JSON: 8 copies of the pixels in every plan read
3. Only ramps (grey-scales plate): no detail, no grain

## Decision

Plate type photo_variations: 2–12 copies (8 by default) of one picture in a grid, each copy an Image layer with its own settings (kind, power, Min power in grayscale, speed, frequency, pulse width, line interval, passes, dot width correction) and under it a grey ramp of the same width at the same settings (a second Image layer, measured step by step); labels above each copy name what differs from the first copy (e.g. 1 J P25 V125 F60 PW200 I.01, 4 J P20, 7 J D0, grayscale as G P2-30). From a photo work or a photo zone the picture is the one the work's file engraves, cut to an 18 mm window where it is opaque and has the most tone, at the size the work engraves it (no shrinking). The picture is kept once as a workshop file (plan pictures, plate-<id>-variation.png, at most 20 px/mm) and every copy's layer refers to it; the plate's geometry holds the copies, labels, picture name, size, hash and source.

## Reason

The ramp measures each copy's tone exactly and the picture its detail and grain, from the same photograph. A window at real size keeps the relation of dots to features the work will have. One stored picture keeps the plan small and the copies identical.

## Consequences

8 copies of 18 mm make a plate about 90 × 65 mm with markers; 18 LightBurn layers. A plate whose picture file is missing cannot be exported again (it says so).
