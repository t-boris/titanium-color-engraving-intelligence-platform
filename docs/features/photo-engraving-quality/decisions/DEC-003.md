## Context

The operator wants decisions to converge: each plate should make the defaults better, not just produce more data.

## Alternatives

1. A full grid of two settings: 8 copies cover only 2 settings × 4 values
2. Random or Bayesian proposals: harder to read on the plate, and 1–2 plates is too little data for a model

## Decision

photo_quality.variants_around(centre, step): the centre, then one change each. A dot kind: power −20 % and +20 %, speed +40 %, line interval ×1.69 (0.01 → 0.0169 mm), the dot width correction switched (0.01 mm on, or off), two other dot kinds (Jarvis ↔ Atkinson ↔ Stucki). Grayscale: Max −20 % and +20 %, Min ±3 points, line interval ×1.5 and ×0.75, speed ×0.7 and ×1.4. Power never above 100 %. The next plate is centred on the best copy of the last one with step halved (0.5: power ±10 %, …), so the steps shrink as the measured best stops moving.

## Reason

One change per copy measures each setting's effect (its sensitivity) from one photograph; halving around the best is a simple, robust search that the operator can follow on the plate's labels.
