## Context

I-6 asks for a Contrast recolouring that improves the shared-border-length-weighted mean ΔE between neighbouring regions over Closest. Pushed alone, that objective takes the most distant titanium colours whatever the picture shows: on test palettes the mean ΔE to the source colours went from 8.5 to 37.7.

## Alternatives

1. Maximise the neighbour ΔE alone (the picture is no longer recognised)
2. A hard cap on each colour's ΔE to its source (one more number to set, and a cap that suits a pale picture fails a bright one)
3. Weigh the source error at half the neighbour gain (tried: the source error rose about fourfold)

## Decision

Contrast starts from Closest (each colour its own entry, DEC-089) and then, colour by colour in index order, swaps two colours' entries or gives a colour an unused entry while that raises the border-weighted mean ΔE between neighbouring source colours (each taken at its own size) less twice the pixel-weighted mean ΔE to the source colours (palette.CONTRAST_FIDELITY = 2.0); passes repeat until no move raises it. The operator's own choices stay and count as fixed neighbours. Borders come from the split (every pair of colours that share an arc, by length). It is deterministic: the same work gives the same variant.

## Reason

A move has to part neighbours twice as much as it strays, so the picture stays recognisable while neighbours that Closest made alike are told apart: on spread palettes the neighbour ΔE about doubled and the source ΔE about doubled from a small value (8.5 to 15). It needs no new setting.

## Consequences

29 colours against 150 measured colours take about 0.3 s. Tests: on three fixtures Contrast beats Closest on the neighbour ΔE and on the score; the same input gives the same answer; kept choices stay; without borders Contrast is Closest.
