## Context

DEC-076 asks for an explicit choice among supported splitting algorithms, each described by the images it is for, and this feature's discussion said the set had to be chosen and tried on sample images before it could be built. The operator then asked for everything left to be finished (2026-10-19). The split makes superpixels through one function and the rest of the pipeline (colour classes, small-region merge, shared-border planar map, export) is independent of how they are made, so every OpenCV superpixel algorithm that ships in the already required opencv-contrib could be plugged in without a new library. Five were run through the real pipeline with the job's own settings on three pictures: the operator's portrait (work 22, 1200 x 1144 px), a photograph of a printed sheet with thin ruled lines and text (900 x 1200 px), and synthetic flat shapes.</context>

## Alternatives

1. Offer all five tried algorithms (SLIC, SLICO, MSLIC, LSC, SEEDS)
2. Keep SLIC only and treat the choice as detail presets
3. Choose the algorithm automatically from the picture

## Decision

Offer two algorithms: SLIC (the default, as built) and SEEDS. The split settings, the creation form and the API carry an algorithm; the document and the history record which made each version; a work made before the choice reads as SLIC. Detail presets are not algorithms and stay a separate choice. Measured (12 colours, medium detail): portrait: SLIC 323 regions, 2.4 s, mean colour error 7.35 (p95 17.4); SEEDS 316 regions, 1.8 s, 7.47 (17.9). Sheet photograph: SLIC 120 regions, 0.93 s, 3.93 (11.4); SEEDS 104 regions, 0.77 s, 3.79 (9.8), and only SEEDS kept the headline legible. On a lit sheet with noise and a dark band, over six settings, SEEDS kept 87-93 % of the thin strokes against SLIC's 83-88 % with 81-114 regions against 276-442. Every algorithm gave 0.000 % uncovered and 0.000 % overlapping area. Not offered: LSC smeared the faces of the portrait (error 7.64, p95 19.5, the man's mouth and eyes lost) and collapsed to one region on a 240 x 180 picture; SLICO gave ragged borders on flat shapes (error 2.30 against 0.81) and no gain elsewhere; MSLIC was never better than SLIC (error 7.48 and 4.01, slower).

## Reason

Two methods with a measured, visible difference serve the operator's "sometimes one, sometimes another, depending on the picture"; the others were worse than SLIC or failed on some picture, and a choice that is never better is clutter. The seam keeps adding a method cheap if a picture needs one.

## Consequences

SEEDS is slightly rougher on portraits (its borders and colour error), so SLIC stays the default. A first measurement of thin strokes with an absolute darkness threshold at 4 colours favoured SLIC; it was replaced by ink measured against its own surroundings at 6 colours, which does not let a grey class pass for ink, and that is the one the test uses. A new algorithm needs the same three-picture check before it is offered.</consequences>
