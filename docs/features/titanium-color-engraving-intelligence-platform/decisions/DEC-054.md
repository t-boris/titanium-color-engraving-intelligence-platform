## Context

The operator engraves photographs in LightBurn's grayscale mode: the grey level of the picture sets the laser power between Min and Max, and the oxide colour on titanium follows the power, so the result is nearly a colour picture. The file holds one image object and no outlines; the frame of the photograph is brushed metal that no dark-frame detector recognises; the exposure is very dark. A first measurement looked at lightness only.

## Alternatives

1. Ask the operator for four corners every time
2. Detect the frame only as a dark window
3. Report lightness alone
4. Model LightBurn's dithering and brightness curves

## Decision

The photograph is registered to the picture stored in the file: edge maps over the three Lab channels (the colour carries more of the picture than the lightness) are correlated over the 8 orientations and a grid of sizes and positions, the best two orientations are refined with ECC from coarse to fine, and the result is refused unless the best orientation beats the second by 1.4 times and the correlation reaches 0.25. The edge of the frame is found from the step of the median level along each side, which gives the margins the frame hides and the visible window. The tonal profile reports, per grey level, the power it implies (linear between Min for the lightest and Max for the darkest, as in LightBurn's documentation) and the measured L*, a*, b* and chroma with their rank correlations. The single median colour of the layer is still measured.

## Reason

The operator proposed finding the silhouettes of the portrait on the photograph; the matching of edge maps is exactly that and works where there are no vectors. On the operator's photograph (IMG_3283 with Inna - 2.lbrn2): orientation margin 2.1 times, window margins 2.9, 7.3, 3.8 and 3.2 mm, rank correlation of grey with L* 0.64 to 0.80, a* -0.64, b* 0.50 to 0.72, a smooth monotone curve from near black at 14 percent power to a light brown at 6 to 7 percent.

## Consequences

Photo engravings align automatically after the vector route refuses. The linear power rule is the documented one; a LightBurn staff reply says the real mapping follows the sRGB curve, which is not verified, so powers are labelled as implied. Measurements are camera-relative without a chart. The picture must be visible enough in the photograph; a refusal falls back to four corners.
