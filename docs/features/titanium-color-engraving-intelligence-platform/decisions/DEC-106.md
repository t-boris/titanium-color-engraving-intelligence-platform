## Context

A free picture of a LightBurn file is measured as a tonal profile: 16 bins of grey against the colour photographed there. The file does not say which edge of the picture is up, so the profile chose the orientation by the rank correlation of grey and lightness and accepted the measurement only when |rho| >= 0.5. On titanium the colours of a scale need not darken step by step (gold, violet, blue ...), as the step-plate profile already states. On a copy of the operator's data a photograph drawn exactly from work 28's scale (150 mm/s, 1-30 %) gave rho -0.14: it was read upside down and dropped as "weak", so its engraving could never check the prediction. Picture registration (picturing.register) had already matched the same picture on the photograph, reflected poses included.

## Alternatives

1. Only for the files of photo works (TCE-P identifier); other works with a picture keep the correlation rule
2. Keep the correlation rule: a work whose scale does not darken step by step is never checked by its photograph

## Decision

When the photograph was aligned by matching the picture of its own file (alignment "picture" or "picture at ruler scale"), every picture of the file is read as it was matched (no second guess of the edge that is up) and the match is the evidence that its greys lie where they were engraved: the tonal profile is "clear". The rank correlation is still reported. Other alignments (markers, outlines, manual) keep the correlation rule. This holds for every work with a picture, not only photo works; accepted pairs keep their stored measurement until re-measured.

## Reason

Registration and measurement must not disagree about the same picture: registration has established the geometry, and guessing it again from lightness reads a non-monotone scale upside down. Confirmed on the copy: the same engraving went from no points to 16 greys, median 2.8 dE, confirmed. Chosen by the operator ("Trust the picture match").

## Consequences

A wrongly registered picture would now be measured as clear; registration already refuses poor matches, and the overlay on the check screen shows the matched silhouettes. A manual correction of the alignment returns to the correlation rule.
