## Context

DEC-037 kept waveforms only as key figures. The user pointed out that the charts shown for the laser do not look like the waveform graphs of the test report (power in kW against time in ns for one pulse), which are what they expect to see for each laser. The two printed graphs (250 ns at 46 kHz and 2 ns at 1950 kHz) have clear axes with printed tick labels.

## Alternatives

1. Show only the photograph of the graph (no data, no comparison with the passport).
2. Draw an idealised pulse shape from the pulse width (it would not be the measured shape and would look authoritative).

## Decision

The curve of each printed waveform is extracted from the photograph by image processing, calibrated on the printed tick labels, and stored as time/power points in the device passport, flagged approximate and linked to the source photograph. The device page draws it with the same axes as the original (P in kW against t in ns). The pulse energy obtained by integrating the curve is shown next to the energy from the passport (rated power divided by frequency) as a check on the digitisation.

## Reason

The operator recognises the original graph and can compare it with the photograph on the same page. The integral of a measured pulse must equal the pulse energy, so a digitisation that disagrees with the passport is visible at once instead of being trusted.

## Consequences

Waveforms exist only for the two conditions printed in the report; other pulse widths show no waveform rather than a guess. A photograph taken at an angle limits the accuracy to a few percent. The measurement was made by the manufacturer with an unknown detector: the ringing after the 2 ns pulse is the detector and cable, not the laser.
