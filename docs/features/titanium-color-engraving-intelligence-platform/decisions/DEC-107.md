## Context

The operator's «Inna Portrait» engraved 150 mm wide came out straw and gold where the 38.4 mm grey ramps of the same settings had given violet and blue (median 14 dE apart). The same file's greys drawn with the colours measured at 150 mm matched the engraving (median 4.9 dE). Small pictures on a rough plate came out darker. Colours had been pooled whatever the size of what was engraved, in photo works, the grey scales and the vector palette alike.

## Alternatives

1. Keep pooling every size and warn only (rejected: the 150 mm portrait was 14 dE off)
2. Use the picture's area or the plate size instead of the row length (rejected: the hypothesis is about the time until the laser returns, which the row sets)
3. Extrapolate beyond the measured sizes (rejected: one size gives no slope; the nearest size is used and marked)
4. Store row_mm in the database (rejected for now: the files give it for old and new pairs alike, and no migration is needed)

## Decision

Every measurement keeps the row length of its layer (row_mm): the length the laser marks along one scan row through it, averaged over its marked points (sum of m_r squared over sum of m_r, 512 sampled rows along the scan angle). A grey ramp gives its width, a picture the width of its largest bitmap, a fill the shapes it fills per row (all shapes at once, LightBurn's default; shape by shape for scanOpt "individual" or flood fill); Line and Offset layers have none. It is derived from the pair's stored LightBurn file and cached by file stamp; nothing is written to the database. Sizes within 10 % are one size. A prediction for rows L mm long uses the measurements of that size; between two measured sizes it interpolates Lab in the logarithm of the length; outside them it takes the nearest size and says so, never extrapolating; with no size known it pools as before. A photo work predicts at its width; an engraving is compared only with others at or around its own size (another size is "not compared", not a failed confirmation). A vector work shows each layer at the row length of its own regions and chooses for each source colour by the colour each recipe gives at the row length of that colour's regions. Plates measure the effect: a ramp-width axis on the grey-ramp plate, a fill-size plate, plates up to 300 mm, and a one-ramp plate at a photo work's width.

## Reason

The working hypothesis is heat: the laser comes back over a point one row later, so short rows return while the titanium is still hot and drive the oxide further. Row length is what changes that time, and it is known exactly from the file. Interpolating in log length follows the hypothesis that heat falls off smoothly with the length; refusing to extrapolate keeps unmeasured sizes honest and points to the plate that measures them.

## Consequences

Most fill colours are measured as 3-8 mm plate patches, so the layers of vector works lie outside the measured sizes and are marked until a fill-size plate is engraved. A missing LightBurn file leaves its measurements without a size. Cross-hatch and "fill by group" are read as one fill. Colour works (discrete colour engraving from knowledge entries) do not use sizes yet.
