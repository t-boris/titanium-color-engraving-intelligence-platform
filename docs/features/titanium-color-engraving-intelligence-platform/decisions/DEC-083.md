## Context

Operator, 2026-10-20: when new experiments and pictures are added the system determines new colours. A setting needs a notion of confidence in its value, and more confident colours are preferred. The same colour repeating under the same settings is more confident; a colour that changes under the same settings is not confident and cannot be taken; if a setting was first yellow and a later redo gives dark brown, the colour is replaced by the dark brown. Until now the colour of a recipe was the plain mean of its independent engravings (model.summarize, palette._entry), so one stale or wrong measurement dragged the mean to a colour nobody measured, and confidence was n/(n+2) whatever the engravings said. On the operator's data (copy of 2026-10-20) 23 of 539 recipes were repeated and 11 of them disagreed by more than ΔE 7 (up to 22.8); the palette of work 22 has 505 recipes, 496 of them engraved once, and 14 settings appear twice or three times because their colours were measured on different bases (chart, against clean titanium, as photographed).

## Alternatives

1. Plain mean of all engravings with a confidence penalised by variance only: keeps the invented in-between colour (rejected)
2. Recency-weighted mean: a newer wrong measurement silently outweighs several agreeing older ones, and the in-between colour remains (rejected)
3. Pool the three measurement bases of one setting into one estimate: REQ-029/DEC-056 keep bases apart because camera-relative colours are not comparable (rejected; the better basis replaces the worse one instead)
4. Exclude every recipe engraved once from the palette: 496 of 505 entries, no vector work could be coloured (rejected)

## Decision

1. Two independent engravings of one recipe AGREE when their colours differ by at most ΔE 5 (CIE76, DEC-026); the limit is an assumption of the model like CONFIDENCE_K, set in one place (agreement.AGREEMENT_DE). 2. The colour of a recipe is the mean of the largest group of engravings that agree with one another (each within ΔE 5 of a representative engraving); engravings outside it do not move the colour. On a tie in size the group holding the newest engraving wins, so a redo replaces an older colour. Re-photographs of one engraving are one engraving (DEC-021). 3. Confidence = k² / ((k + 2) · n), k the engravings in the agreeing group, n all independent engravings. With all engravings agreeing it is n/(n+2) as before (0.5 for two, 0.6 for three); every engraving that differs lowers it; it is not assessed (null) for one engraving, which ranks as 1/3, between a contradicted recipe and a repeated one. A recipe is CONTRADICTED when n ≥ 2 and the agreeing group is not a strict majority (2k ≤ n). 4. Stability, variance and expected ΔE keep being computed over all engravings, so disagreement still shows. 5. The palette of a vector work: an entry measured on a worse basis than another entry of the same settings (chart > against clean titanium > as photographed; same laser, lens, titanium family and grade, mode and task) is SUPERSEDED. Contradicted and superseded entries stay in the palette (saved works keep their ids and may keep them by hand) but the automatic modes do not choose them. Within the delta E tolerance the more confident entry is chosen before the faster one (this orders DEC-075's fastest-within-tolerance by confidence first). 6. When no entry is free of caution (every colour of the titanium changed, or was replaced) the automatic modes use the cautioned ones so that a work can still be coloured; each is marked. 7. The colour map draws the circle of a colour with a diameter that follows its recipe's confidence (radius 3 + 12 × confidence, one engraving counting 1/3). 8. The colour map search measures a recipe's distance to its agreed colour, not to its nearest single point, and shows its confidence and agreement. Measurements are never changed or deleted (REQ-001): everything is derived on read.

## Reason

It follows the operator's rules literally and is explainable on the screen as a count (k of n engravings agree): repetition builds confidence, difference lowers it, a redo replaces an old colour, and an outlier cannot invent a colour nobody measured. A median or a recency-weighted mean would blend yellow and dark brown into a colour that never occurred; a fixed ΔE limit separates the real data cleanly (agreeing repeats ΔE 0.8–4.1, contradicting 7–22.8).

## Consequences

The mean of a recipe whose engravings disagree by more than ΔE 5 changes to the agreed colour; palette ids, which hash the recipe, do not change. REQ-002's rule that a metric not supported is unavailable still holds: confidence stays null for one engraving. A recipe with two disagreeing engravings is cheaper to fix than to explain: engrave it once more, the third measurement decides. The limit ΔE 5 and the order of confidence before time can be changed in one place each.
