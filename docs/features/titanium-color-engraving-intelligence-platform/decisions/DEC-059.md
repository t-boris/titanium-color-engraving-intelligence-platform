## Context

The operator asked why the black layer of a work measures L* 28 ("why no black"). The black oxide itself is a dark grey (reflectance about 5 %), but the grey ramp of the ColorChecker showed that the chart correction distorted the dark tones: after the 3x3 matrix the black patch came out L* 13 instead of 21.7 and the mid grey 46.5 instead of 51.2 (mean residual 6.0–6.5 dE on six photographs). The RAW was developed to 8 bits with a tone curve and then read as sRGB; the dark patch values of 7–8 of 255 were linearised with the wrong curve, up to threefold too dark.

## Alternatives

1. Keep the 8-bit decode and add an offset term to the matrix (affine fit): black patch overshoots to L* 31–33, residual 5.1–5.6 dE
2. Fit a tone curve to the grey ramp before the matrix: possible, but fits the symptom; the 8-bit quantisation of the darks stays
3. Develop the RAW a second time in 16 bits without a tone curve and measure in that linear image (chosen)

## Decision

Every colour measurement (chart patches, chart-corrected colours, camera-relative colours, the ratio to clean titanium, tonal profiles) is taken from a 16-bit linear development of the RAW (camera white balance, no brightness change, no tone curve). The 24 chart patches are read from the linear image inside the detected outline (6 x 4 grid, central part of each cell); the 3x3 matrix is fitted in linear light as before, with the same residual gate of 12 dE. The 8-bit gamma-encoded decode is kept for chart detection, registration and previews. The check reports the corrected grey ramp beside the reference so that any tone distortion is visible. Non-RAW test images are linearised from sRGB, so their behaviour is unchanged.

## Reason

Measured on the six chart photographs of 2026-10-18: the correction residual falls from 6.0–6.5 to 3.6–4.2 dE and the six grey patches come out within 1–3 L* of the reference at every step (black 22–25 vs 21.7). Chromaticity hardly changes; lightness of dark layers rises by about 8–11 L* (black layers L* 25–28 -> 35–38), of mid tones by 4–8, of light tones by 2–5.

## Consequences

Observations accepted before this decision (pairs 30–37, chart-corrected, and every camera-relative pair) carry the old dark-tone bias and are not comparable with new ones; they should be measured again as new pairs. Checks take about one second longer (second development of the RAW). The nominal clean titanium derived from calibrated witnesses changes accordingly once new witnesses are accepted.
