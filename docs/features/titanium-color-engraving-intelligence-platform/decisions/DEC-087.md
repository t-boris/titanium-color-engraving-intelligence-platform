## Context

Operator, 2026-10-20: the work should go the other way round: the 24 squares are known colours; take the colour from the photo like an eyedropper, find it among the squares and give it numbers from them; "lightening and darkening - I cannot understand why". The global 3x3 matrix of DEC-025/DEC-059 has no offset and is one formula for every colour: on check 60 (IMG_3327) the chart black came out L* 30.4 against the real 21.7 and the grey ramp 94.3/80.5/66.7/52.9/40.4/30.4 against 95.8/80.6/65.9/51.2/36.1/21.7. DEC-085 (eyedropper on the camera's embedded preview) was measured and rejected: the preview reads the chart white at L* 72-91 and the black at 1-15, 20 L* darker than the real chart, and it changes with the camera's exposure.

## Alternatives

1. Keep the global 3x3 matrix (DEC-025): leave-one-square-out error mean 4.5 dE, 5.0 on the dark squares (L* < 40), worst 12.9
2. Affine matrix with an offset fitted in Lab: 3.8 mean, 3.5 on the dark squares, worst 8.5 (13 photographs)
3. Radial-basis look-up over all squares: 4.5-4.7 mean, 6.1 on the dark squares (overshoots between squares; 13 photographs)
4. Eyedropper on the camera preview (DEC-085): rejected, see context

## Decision

A photographed colour (linear RGB of the 16-bit decode) is mapped to a Lab colour in two steps. (1) One smooth map for the whole range: a 3x3 matrix plus an offset, refined to minimise the colour difference in Lab over the 24 squares. (2) What that map misses at the squares nearest to the colour (the 4 nearest in exposure-normalised, gamma-encoded RGB; inverse squared distance, exact at a square) is added, fading to nothing far from every square. The photograph is mapped through this look-up (a 41-node table per channel, trilinear) and the layer colour is still the median of the mapped pixels of its area. The chart residual the screens show is the honest one: each square is hidden in turn and guessed from the other 23. A colour is never lightened or darkened by anything else: the exposure normalisation is the chart's white square seen as 1, as before. The global matrix stays selectable (photo.CALIBRATION = "matrix").

## Reason

It is what the operator described and it measures better where the operator sees the mismatch: leave-one-square-out error 3.6 dE against 4.5 (dark squares 3.1 against 5.0, worst case 8.8 against 12.9) over the 15 charted photographs of the operator's data that have a plausible chart (some are copies of one photograph); on check 60 the grey ramp lands within 0.3 L* on all six squares and the black at 21.7.

## Consequences

The look-up is only as good as the 24 squares: a colour far outside them (very saturated, or a specular layer above the white square) falls back to the smooth map, and layers brighter than the white square (C05, C07, C10 on check 60) still saturate at L* 98-99. Observations already accepted keep their colours (immutable) until the experiment is measured again. The correction fit takes 0.5 s and the whole-picture map about 0.4 s more than the matrix, at check time only. The ΔE 5 agreement of DEC-083 is not changed by this. Supersedes DEC-085 (eyedropper on the camera preview) and the "3x3 matrix" part of DEC-025/DEC-059; the 16-bit linear decode of DEC-059 stays.
