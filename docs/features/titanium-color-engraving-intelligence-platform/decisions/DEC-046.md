## Context

The operator wants controlled experiments where two laser parameters vary while the remaining settings stay fixed. The normal LightBurn Material Test follows that design and can vary Power, Speed, Interval, Passes, Frequency and Q-Pulse when supported. The platform, however, must return one ready-to-open `.lbrn2` containing titanium provenance, registration and the mandatory cleaning pass before any test marks.

## Decision

Provide a Material-Test-style matrix plate. The operator selects two different axes from power, speed, frequency, Q-Pulse width, line interval and layer passes; sets the range and point count of each axis; fixes the other process settings; and chooses vector-line samples or filled patches. Line interval requires filled patches because it controls scan spacing. The default is a 5 × 5 vector-line matrix. The grid may use up to 28 cells; together with clean and registration this uses at most LightBurn output colors 00–29. Keep the default matrix compact (about 60 × 61 mm for a 5 × 5 plate): use 4 mm ArUco corner marks and place the QR in the top registration band instead of reserving a footer. The cleaning rectangle covers only this registered footprint. Larger grids stay within 100 × 100 mm.

Keep separate line-oriented plate types for power/speed threshold, line continuity and line separation. Do not infer an optical spot diameter from the visible engraved trace width.

Export the generated test, labels, registration and clean-first pass together as a standard `.lbrn2`; do not require the operator to edit or combine another file. Preserve selected axis ranges, fixed settings, laser, lens, titanium stock, geometry and measurements in the experiment record.

## Reason

This borrows LightBurn's experimentally useful two-axis comparison while keeping the complete operation reproducible and tied to the exact laser/lens/material tuple. Vector strokes measure line response without using repeated filled swatches for unrelated capability tests.

## Consequences

The plate dialog exposes all six axes and the two geometry modes. Interval matrices switch to filled patches. All generated output includes cleaning first and is checked against the 30-color and 100 mm limits. Line-specific outcomes can be analyzed separately from filled-region outcomes.
