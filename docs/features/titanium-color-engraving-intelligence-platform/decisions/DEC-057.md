## Context

Q-024 asked whether LightBurn turns the grey level of a grayscale-mode image into power linearly or along the sRGB curve (the documentation and a staff reply disagreed). The operator answered in the conversation (2026-10-16): the photo engraving's definition has a minimum and a maximum power in its settings, and the grey level corresponds to the power between them. The operator also agreed that the 14 grey-to-power steps of the portrait, which have no clean titanium in their photograph, show on the colour map by default.

## Alternatives

1. Engrave a grayscale wedge beside flat fills to test linear against sRGB
2. Keep the unreferenced colours hidden by default

## Decision

1) The power of a grey step is Min + (Max - Min) x (1 - grey), where Min and Max are the layer's own minPower and maxPower: the lightest grey gets Min, the darkest Max, linearly between. The unverified-sRGB caveat is removed from the tonal profile; no wedge plate is added. 2) The colour map shows every measured colour by default. A colour with no clean titanium in its photograph (no cleaning layer, no chart) is drawn as photographed, pale with a dotted outline and flagged "без привязки к чистому титану"; a checkbox hides such colours. They do not take part in the nearest-colour search unless the search is asked to include them, because their distances to referenced colours mean nothing; the search says how many were left out.

## Reason

The operator knows the settings of their own files and the rule matches the files read (portrait: Min 5, Max 16.1, grayscale). A complete map is the operator's request; hiding measured colours by default contradicted it, while mixing unreferenced distances into the search would mislead.

## Consequences

Supersedes the part of DEC-056 and REQ-029 that left colours without a witness off the map unless asked for; everything else in them stands. If the engraved result later disagrees with the linear rule, the table of grey to power to colour shows it, since it prints the measured colour of every step beside the power. Photographs of plates come one at a time; whether to obtain a ColorChecker is left to the operator.
