## Context

The manufacturer's table gives a cut-off frequency per pulse width and a figure (Fig. 3) showing output power rising linearly with repetition frequency up to the cut-off and flat above it. Public JPT documentation and a LightBurn forum thread describe the same behaviour (at the cut-off the laser gives its rated power; below it the pulse energy stays constant and average power falls in proportion to frequency). The unit's own test report measured 62.6 W at 200 ns and 48 kHz, which is exactly the table's cut-off point for 200 ns.

## Alternatives

1. Do not model power at all and leave the values to the operator.
2. Fit the droop from power-meter measurements (none exist yet).

## Decision

Output power = rated power x set power fraction x min(1, frequency / cut-off frequency); pulse energy = output power / frequency; areal dose = output power / (speed x line interval x passes^-1) per pass count. The set power is assumed linear. Every figure is shown as a passport model, not a measurement, and a CW setting has no cut-off.

## Reason

Pulse energy and areal dose are the quantities that decide how much heat reaches the titanium and therefore the oxide thickness, which is what the colour depends on; they are the natural inputs for the later AI model. The model is exact at the one point the test report measured and cheap to replace by a measured curve.

## Consequences

Linearity of the power setting is not verified and a power meter reading should replace it. Spot size is unknown, so fluence and pulse overlap are not reported yet (see the open question on the lens).
