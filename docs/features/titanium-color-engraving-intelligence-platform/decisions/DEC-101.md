## Context

DEC-075 estimated time per square centimetre as 100 / (speed x interval) plus 0.3 ms per line of 10 mm, an engineering default until timed against a real job. The operator timed one: Inna + Boris took about 3 h at 150 mm and 1.5-2 h scaled to 100 mm in LightBurn. The 100 mm file estimated 1.2-1.4 h: scaling to two thirds keeps 45 % of the area (marking time) but 67 % of the lines, and its small shapes cut every line into several pieces (1.1 M pieces, not the 0.3 M a 10 mm line assumes).

## Alternatives

1. Keep 0.3 ms per 10 mm line (100 mm job off by 20-40 %)
2. Raise the per-line cost without counting pieces (cannot fit both sizes)
3. A field for real times and a fit over them (more data, later)

## Decision

For a LightBurn file the turnaround is counted per piece of scan line of its shapes: the summed extent of every edge (holes included) across the scan lines, over twice the interval, at the layer's scan angle and, with cross-hatch, also at 90 degrees; a picture gives one piece per row. A recipe without geometry keeps pieces of 10 mm per square centimetre. The cost of a piece is 1 ms (TURNAROUND_S), timed on the operator's jobs: 150 mm 2.9-3.3 h against about 3 h, 100 mm 1.5-1.65 h against 1.5-2 h. The marking time and the one-way return jumps stay as in DEC-075.

## Reason

The count of pieces follows from the geometry exactly on average without drawing a line, and one cost per piece fits both real jobs; 0.3 ms with 10 mm lines fitted the 150 mm job only by chance (its shapes average about 10 mm).

## Consequences

Recipe times per square centimetre (palette, scale cards) grow slightly: 0.0169 mm about +1 %, 0.002 mm about +12 %. Travel between separate shapes is still not counted. Replaces the 0.3 ms default of DEC-075.
