## Context

The experiments screen listed plates, engravings with photographs, and the conclusions for the material in three separate columns. The operator wants experiments as one thing: open one and find the plate it was made from, the engraving, every photograph, the measured result and its conclusions. The plate is used once; templates stay as a way to create one, and the plate can be changed afterwards (by hand or by asking the assistant). The photograph with its overlay must be shown every time, not hidden.

## Alternatives

1. A stored experiment table with a migration
2. Keep three columns and add links between them

## Decision

An experiment is derived, not stored: a plate with all the checks made on it (key plan-id), or a finished work with its photographs (key eng-id; re-photographs share an engraving id). The experiments screen is one list with a thumbnail of the latest photograph, the state, laser, lens and titanium, search and a state filter, a compact working-task panel on top and the conclusions for the material beside it. Opening an experiment shows first the photograph with the overlay (contours of the file, the silhouettes of the stored picture, the visible window, the inferred part under a frame), then every check with its state, the measured layers, the plate, the file and the conclusions. Old routes (plate, check, analysis) keep working and link back to their experiment.

## Reason

The data already links plates to checks (plan id) and checks to one engraving (engraving id), so no schema change and no migration of the operator's data is needed. One list removes the need to know whether something is a plate, an engraving or a photograph.

## Consequences

A plate with no photograph is an experiment waiting for a photograph; several checks of one plate are one experiment; a plate that was deleted leaves its checks as a finished work. The latest check is shown first, the others are one click away.
