## Context

Operator, 2026-10-20: the colours listed for check 60 do not match the photograph, "especially the dark ones"; "why can't you take the colour from the photo like an eyedropper? Why do you set it to some colour of your own, lighten it?" Measured on pair 60 (copy of the data, read only): the camera's own picture (the DNG's embedded JPEG preview, the picture the operator sees) reads the chart white at L 71.9 (reference 95.8) and the grey ramp 71.9/59.2/44.3/30.3/18.6/9.5 (reference 95.8/80.6/65.9/51.2/36.1/21.7). Layer colours, mean L*: eyedropper on that picture 61.3; one exposure gain so that the chart white is white 81.0; the chart fit of DEC-059 79.8; an affine fit 80.7 (C16, the darkest: 36 / 56 / 55 / 53). The lightening is the normalisation of the exposure to the chart white, not the 3x3 matrix. The embedded preview of all 17 photographs on file has the size and geometry of the app's RAW decode (shift 0-2 px at 3000-5000 px).

## Alternatives

1. Keep the chart-corrected linear decode of DEC-059 (comparable between photographs, but lighter than the picture by about 20 L* and clipped on the specular plate)
2. Exposure only: one gain from the chart white, no matrix (same lightness as the chart fit)
3. Both numbers on every layer, the operator picks the one the palette and the map use (recommended by the agent; the operator chose the eyedropper alone)
4. Affine chart fit in Lab (closer to the chart, still not the picture)

## Decision

The colour of a layer is the median of its pixels in the camera's own picture, as the operator sees it: the embedded preview of the RAW (same size and geometry as the app's decode; when it is missing or does not fit, the RAW developed with the camera white balance and automatic brightness), and the image itself for a non-RAW test image. The reference chart is still looked for and read as evidence only: how the picture shows its white, grey ramp and black (an exposure indicator); it is no longer needed to measure a colour and its correction is not applied. The measurement mode is "camera_picture" and counts as a standard (absolute) measurement everywhere "reference_chart_corrected" did; observations measured with the chart stay valid as the basis "chart" until they are measured again. The basis is the setting measurement_basis ("picture", the default of the application, or "chart"). The photograph views of a check show the same picture that is measured.

## Reason

The operator wants the colour in the list to be the colour they see on the picture; the eyedropper is that, and every step between the picture and the number (exposure normalisation, matrix, clipping) was a place where the number could part from the picture. The numbers above show the size of the gap (about 20 L*).

## Consequences

The camera's own exposure is not steady: the chart white reads L 72-91 over 14 photographs (median 82.5), so the same recipe photographed twice can differ by several L*; the agreement rule of DEC-083 (dE 5) will then count some repeats as differing, and colours of different photographs are comparable only as far as the exposure is. Fix the exposure when shooting (lock it), or add a normalisation later by a decision of its own; the chart reading stored with every check shows how far a photograph is from the others. The 678 observations measured with the chart are a different basis until the experiments are measured again (a re-measure of an experiment is part of the same release). DEC-059 stays for the chart detection, the as-shot decode and the chart evidence; it no longer decides the colour of a layer. Clipping at the chart white no longer happens, the picture's own tone curve (the camera's rendering) is part of the number.
