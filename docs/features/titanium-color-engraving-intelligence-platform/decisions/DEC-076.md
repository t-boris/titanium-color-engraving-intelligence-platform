## Context

The operator asked that a photograph is split 'as beautifully as possible' into vectors that touch each other tightly, without gaps, with eyes and lips most important. Simplifying each region's outline on its own (as the first vectoriser did) moves shared borders apart and leaves gaps or overlaps.

## Alternatives

1. Simplify every region outline independently (gaps and overlaps)
2. Pixel-exact outlines without smoothing (no gaps, but jagged)
3. A new vectorising library

## Decision

The image is smoothed edge-preservingly, over-segmented into superpixels (OpenCV ximgproc SLIC, already a dependency) whose size is smaller in important areas (eyes, lips, marked areas), and the superpixels are clustered in Lab into the chosen number of colours; regions under the minimum area are merged into the neighbour of nearest colour, except in important areas. The label map is turned into a planar map: every border between two regions is one arc between junctions, simplified and smoothed once and used by both regions, so the regions cover the picture exactly, without gaps or overlaps. Holes are kept. Export writes every region as a closed Path from its arcs.

## Reason

Shared arcs are the only way two neighbouring outlines stay identical after smoothing; superpixels follow edges, which gives regions that follow the picture.

## Consequences

Tested by rasterising the exported regions: their union covers the picture and they overlap only on their shared borders. Proposed for the operator's review as the method of REQ-032.
