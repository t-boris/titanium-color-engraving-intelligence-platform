## Context

REQ-004 says Tonal maps luminance to a monotonic parameter curve judged by brightness and contrast, and Color-path maps luminance to a user-chosen trajectory in titanium color space judged by delta E along the path. It does not say how a luminance value becomes a laser parameter set, and no measured data exists between two measured parameter sets.

## Alternatives

1. Interpolate power, frequency and speed linearly between two measured sets. The color between them is not measured, so this would present a guess as a calibrated prediction.
2. Fit a continuous model of color over the parameters and invert it. This needs far more measured data than a first version has.

## Decision

Both sub-modes use one transfer-function abstraction: an ordered list of measured knowledge entries, each with a position between 0 and 1. A pixel belongs to band floor(u * N) (the last band includes u = 1). A band takes the entry whose position is nearest to its middle (i + 0.5) / N; ties go to the lower position. Parameters are never interpolated, so every band uses a parameter set that was measured.

- Tonal: eligible entries are sorted by measured lightness L; positions are their lightness normalised between the darkest and the brightest entry. A pixel's luminance u is its Lab L divided by 100 (0 to 1) and is used directly as the position on the curve, so the full tonal range of the image is stretched onto the measured range; pixels are never matched against absolute measured lightness. At least two entries with different L are required. The curve is monotonic in measured lightness by construction. It is judged by the planned and measured brightness (L) and by contrast, defined as the difference between the largest and the smallest L across the bands.
- Color-path: the operator chooses an ordered trajectory of at least two distinct eligible entries. Positions follow the cumulative delta E along the polyline of their Lab values. It is judged by delta E between each band's measured color and its planned color and by the distance of the measured color from the trajectory polyline.
- Bands without pixels still become layers, so N layers are exported for N bands.

## Reason

It keeps every exported layer tied to a parameter set with measurements behind it, and it gives both sub-modes one pipeline, one preview and one export path as DEC-007 asks.

## Consequences

Proposed only. The tonal range is limited to the measured lightness range, and a smooth gradient is approximated by at most as many distinct tones as there are eligible entries, however large N is. More measurements widen and refine the curve.
