## Context

Three new pairs (CowBoy, lion, limitless, 2026-10-18) were refused by the automatic alignment. Two failed because the frame-window detector found no window: black parts of the work touched the frame and the line tolerance did not scale with the photograph. The lion was refused because the orientation cost (a trimmed mean over the best half of the outline points) could not tell the orientations of a dense drawing of parallel strokes apart, and the old whole-shape fallback was then accepted with three shapes and no independent check. The operator asked why the frame is an input at all when every section of the file can be found on its own and the plate size is known.

## Alternatives

1. Keep the frame window as the start and make its detector more tolerant (dominant line, scaled tolerance): fixes CowBoy and limitless, still fails any unframed or glossy-framed photograph
2. Find every element of the file independently by shape (the old whole-shape route) and grow from the found ones: ambiguous on dense drawings of similar elements, no independent check
3. Search the whole drawing over every pose in a small copy of the photograph, with edge directions, then grow element anchors from that start; derive the frame afterwards from the plate rectangle (chosen)

## Decision

The registration of an unmarked work takes only the file geometry and the photograph. The whole drawing is searched over mirror, four right angles with small offsets, the plate's scale and every shift in a 240 px copy, correlating outline samples with a direction-aware proximity map of the photograph's edges (8 direction bins), so that a drawing of parallel strokes cannot match itself turned by a right angle. Every orientation's best pose is polished at 1100 px through a pyramid of distance caps (3, 1.5, 0.8 mm) and compared by a direction-aware cost; the orientation must win by the margin 0.9 and, after the element anchoring, by the anchor count. The element anchoring, the RANSAC fit and the gates (anchors, inlier fraction, residual, spread) are unchanged. Only then is the frame looked for: along every side of the plate rectangle (known size) the inner edge of a dark frame is the dominant line where a dark run ends; a side without such an edge is the plate edge; a photograph without a frame hides nothing. A registration found by another route (whole shapes) is only a start and holds only if the element anchoring confirms it; a file too simple for anchors keeps the whole-shape result.

## Reason

Nothing physical that may be absent (frame, chart, marker) may stop the measurement of the operator's work; the plate size in the file already says where the frame can be. Measured on the real pairs: unbreakable 72 anchors, 0.066 mm median residual; lion 26 anchors, 0.077 mm; CowBoy 27 anchors, 0.072 mm; limitless 41 anchors, 0.123 mm; frame margins per side 0.5–7 mm, consistent with the photographs. Synthetic: an unframed work registers and hides nothing; a black silhouette on two plate edges leaves the frame edges where they are.

## Consequences

DEC-049 keeps its meaning (the visible window limits what is measured) but the window is an output of the registration, with a per-side flag whether a frame edge was found; the alignment record gains window_found and the search diagnostics; the UI says when no frame was found. The whole-shape route is reported as "element anchors" when confirmed and is refused with a reason when not. The registration takes 8–9 s per pair. Pair 23 (lion) was accepted on the unconfirmed whole-shape fit, off by 0.4 mm median and up to 2 mm at the edges; its 19 observations should be re-checked.
