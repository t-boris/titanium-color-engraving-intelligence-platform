## Statement

The system shall support undo and redo for every colouring and geometry operation in the photograph vector work editor by restoring snapshots of colouring, document version, ΔE tolerance and filters. New operations shall clear redo history, and newly created document versions shall use non-repeating numbers one greater than the highest in history. Variants shall be restorable only on their recorded document version. Splitting again shall clear undo, redo and variants. Undo entries from older works shall remain usable and be treated as belonging to their current document version.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] Undo and redo cover every colouring and geometry operation, including painting, merging titanium colours, merging source colours and merging neighbouring areas.
- [ ] Each undo/redo snapshot holds the colouring, document version, ΔE tolerance and filters in force; restoring a snapshot restores all four.
- [ ] Undoing a merge restores its earlier document version, including its geometry, from history; redoing the merge restores the merged version.
- [ ] Undo after paint, merge of colours, merge of areas and paint returns step by step to the first state, document version and colouring; redo returns step by step to the last.
- [ ] Undoing and redoing a change of ΔE tolerance or filters restores the corresponding tolerance, filters and colouring.
- [ ] A new editor operation after an undo clears all redo steps.
- [ ] Each newly created document version receives a number one greater than the highest version number in the history, including redo history before it is cleared; version numbers are never reused.
- [ ] Each variant records the document version on which it was created.
- [ ] Restoring a variant made on another document version is refused with a translated message; it can be restored once undo brings its recorded document version back.
- [ ] Splitting the photograph again starts fresh and clears undo history, redo history and all variants.
- [ ] Undo entries of works made before this release are treated as belonging to the work's current document version and remain undoable.
