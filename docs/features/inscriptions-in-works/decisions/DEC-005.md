## Context

A text of several lines can be laid on an arc, a circle or a path. A line below the first is a line spacing away from the curve, so the curve it really runs on is shorter (inside a bend) or longer (outside one). Placing its letters by the first line's lengths crowded them together in the middle of a circle (upright letters overlapped) or squeezed them (bent letters), as seen on a copy of work 40 with «Инна и Борис / שלום» on a circle.

## Alternatives

1. Bend the whole block as one envelope (every line takes the first line's angles): inner lines shrink or overlap
2. Allow only one line on a curve: the operator would have to make each ring a separate inscription

## Decision

Every line runs on the curve parallel to the first at its height (on an arc or a circle, the circle about the same centre; on a path, the parallel curve, whose length is the path's less the height times its turn), its letters spaced along it as on a straight line and aligned on the same anchor. A rectangle or oval backing is bent with the first line and spans every line. A text on an arc or a circle is said not to fit (overflow, a warning on the screen) when a line is longer than its own circle or letters reach past the centre; a path is the operator's drawing and is not checked.

## Reason

The letter height and spacing the operator sets are kept on every line, so a medallion of several concentric lines reads as designed, and a layout that cannot work (a line past the centre) is named instead of drawn silently.

## Consequences

Inner lines on a circle span a larger angle than the first line; lines inside a tight bend of a path stop where the parallel curve would run back.
